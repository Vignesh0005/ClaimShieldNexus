# Model card: ClaimShield FWA classifier v1.0.1

> **Synthetic-trained model. Not validated for production use.** Generated by `scripts/train_xgboost.py` on 2026-10-08. Do not edit by hand.

## Model name and version

- **Name:** ClaimShield FWA classifier (`xgboost`), with isolation forest and local outlier factor anomaly scores
- **Version:** v1.0.1
- **Features:** 16 (see `app/detection/ml/feature_engineering.py`)
- **Explanations:** per-claim TreeSHAP attributions

## Training data

2,981 synthetic claims, of which 14.3% carry a planted billing pattern (duplicate billing, upcoding, unbundling, excessive utilisation, impossible day, referral ring, services with no encounter, identity misuse). No real claims and no protected health information. Labels come from the planting script, plus any reviewer decisions recorded in the system at retraining time.

## IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS

- Trained on synthetic claims with planted patterns; performance on production claims is unverified.
- Reported metrics are high because the planted patterns are easy to separate. They are not a forecast of real-world accuracy.
- The model relies heavily on provider volume features, so it partly recognises busy providers instead of billing behaviour. See the provider-split figures.
- Peer comparison uses specialty only and does not adjust for case mix or practice size.
- Labels come from planted patterns and reviewer decisions, which can carry reviewer bias.
- No bias audit across provider or member groups has been run on real data.
- The random-split figures are inflated: claims from the same provider appear in both training and test data, so the model can recognise the provider. Use the provider-split column.
- With only 8 providers carrying planted patterns, the provider-split figures are themselves unstable and would change with a different synthetic seed.

## Intended use

Ranks healthcare claims and provider clusters for human review by a Special Investigations Unit. A score indicates how closely a claim resembles patterns reviewers have referred before. It is not a finding of fraud, waste or abuse.

Not intended for:

- Automatically denying, pending or recouping claims
- Decisions about a member's eligibility or care
- Use without review by a qualified investigator

## Bias analysis (which features dominate)

- The three most influential features are provider_members_30d (63%), provider_claims_30d (8%), has_referrer (5%).
- Provider volume and billing-level features (provider_avg_claim, provider_claims_30d, provider_members_30d, provider_peer_ratio) account for 74% of total gain. A model that leans on them tends to score busy providers higher whatever they bill.
- Member age is a model input. Its effect on scores for older or younger members has not been measured.
- F1 falls from 0.95 on a random split to 0.85 on providers the model never saw (a gap of 0.10), which shows how much it relies on recognising providers.

## Fairness considerations

Not yet measured. No analysis has been run of score or false-positive differences across provider groups (rural or urban, solo or group practice, specialty) or member groups (age, low-income area). This must be done on real, reviewed cases before any production use.
